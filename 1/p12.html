Budget 2027, read against the evidence

A month after the budget, the rating agency and the Commission gave their verdicts. They agree on more than the headlines suggest.

Valletta, seen through limestone arches · Image: Corporate ID Group

The budget presented on 26 October kept the energy shield, gave families with children the second year of tax cuts and asked visitors, non-EU workers and non-residents to pay more. The Malta Observer’s reading puts the deficit at 2.6% of GDP in 2027 and debt at 46.8% by the end of that year, with €400m going to energy and fuel subsidies and €324m expected from the new revenue measures.

November then brought two outside verdicts. On 2 November Moody’s kept Malta at A2 with a stable outlook. Sustained growth and a diversified services sector, it said, have helped the country withstand international shocks; continued energy subsidies, higher social security spending and growing public investment will push the deficit up. It also highlighted persistent weaknesses in the rule of law, judicial independence and corruption control, and limited capacity in the public administration.

On 17 November the European Commission’s autumn forecast, reported in full on pages 10–11, put growth at 3.9% this year, 3.5% in 2027 and 3.8% in 2028, after 4.7% in 2025, with the deficit at 2.8% of GDP until 2028 and debt rising to 47.2%. It called the commitment to freeze retail energy prices a major uncertainty.

What the forecasts agree on

The three forecasters differ on the pace, not the direction. Growth stays strong by European standards, but slows; the deficit widens as subsidies and tax cuts bite; and the labour that powered the last decade costs more. The Commission expects higher work-permit fees to slow labour-force growth to 3.2%.
