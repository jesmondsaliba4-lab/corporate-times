The space between us

Why the next renaissance must begin with human relationship.

Essay II · Jesmond Saliba · 25 October 2026 · Abridged
Human and artificial intelligence, facing the Vitruvian figure · Image: ThinkPeople Nexus · Enquire.Nexus

We are living through an extraordinary expansion of intelligence. Systems can interpret information, identify patterns, generate ideas, translate languages and support decisions at a speed that would have been difficult to imagine even a few years ago.

The question is no longer whether artificial intelligence will become part of our lives. It already has. The more important question is what kind of life we are creating around it.

I do not believe the future depends on choosing between people and technology. But I do believe it depends on understanding what each can contribute — and what neither should be expected to replace in the other.

Because there is something between people that cannot be reduced to information passing from one point to another. It is where trust forms. Where meaning is negotiated. Where disagreement can be held without becoming separation. Where someone feels listened to rather than merely processed. And where knowledge gradually becomes judgement.

Connected, but not necessarily in relationship

We have more ways to communicate than any generation before us. But connection and relationship are not the same thing. A connection can transmit information. A relationship carries context. It remembers what was said, but also recognises what could not yet be said.

Technology can reinforce the pressure to be faster because it often gives us something that appears complete: a recommendation, a summary, a probability, a confident response. But confidence is not the same as wisdom. And the best available answer is not automatically the right decision.

Someone must still ask what the answer means for the people affected by it. Someone must recognise what the data does not show. Those responsibilities remain human.

The renaissance we now require

The Renaissance did not advance by insisting that every discipline remain inside its own boundary. It created spaces in which different forms of understanding could meet. Our own age requires a different renaissance: technology with purpose, intelligence with judgement, capability with responsibility, innovation with its human consequences.
