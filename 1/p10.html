Malta’s economy to grow by 3.9% in 2026, but deficit and debt set to rise

European Commission forecasts slower growth in 2027 as global headwinds, higher inflation and changes in labour migration weigh on the outlook. Government’s €1,000 annual payment to eligible workers expected to boost consumption, while deficit is projected to remain at 2.8% of GDP through 2028.

cde.news · 17 November 2026

Malta’s economy is expected to continue expanding at a robust pace, although growth is forecast to moderate next year amid a challenging international environment, while rising government expenditure is set to push the budget deficit higher and increase public debt.

In its Autumn 2026 Economic Forecast, published on 17 November, the European Commission projects Malta’s real Gross Domestic Product (GDP) to grow by 3.9% in 2026, before slowing to 3.5% in 2027 and recovering slightly to 3.8% in 2028.

The Commission attributes the country’s continued economic resilience to strong domestic demand, robust private expenditure and investment, and the performance of its services sectors, particularly tourism, recreational, information technology, financial and professional services.

Malta recorded real economic growth of 4.7% in 2025, with global uncertainty so far having had little material impact on its performance.

However, the Commission expects a combination of global economic headwinds, a moderation in labour migration and the gradual maturation of some fast-growing services sectors to weigh on growth in 2027.

The Government’s fiscal measures for next year are also expected to influence the composition of economic activity, boosting private consumption but contributing to lower net exports and higher inflation.

Government measures to boost private consumption

Private consumption is forecast to grow by 4% in 2026, accelerating to 4.8% in 2027, before moderating to 4% in 2028. The increase next year is expected to be supported by an annual tax-free payment of €1,000 by the Government to eligible Maltese workers.

The Commission expects the additional household spending to increase demand for imported goods and services, thereby reducing the contribution of net exports to economic growth.

At the same time, higher tourism taxes are expected to have a moderate negative impact on the growth of services exports.

The combined effect of these measures is expected to be broadly neutral on overall real GDP growth, despite their impact on the composition of economic activity.

Government consumption is also expected to provide continued support to the economy. Having increased by 7.5% in 2026, it is forecast to moderate to 2.7% in 2027 before accelerating again to 5.4% in 2028.

Investment, following a 0.4% increase in 2025, is projected to expand by 3% in 2026, 3.9% in 2027 and 3% in 2028, with public investment expected to be the main driver.

The Commission expects the contribution of net exports to remain positive throughout the forecast period, supported by Malta’s substantial surplus in services trade, which more than offsets its deficit in goods trade. However, the contribution is expected to weaken in 2027 before improving again in 2028.

Employment growth continues to rely on foreign workers

Employment growth is expected to accelerate to 4.2% in 2026, compared with 3.9% in 2025, with the expansion continuing to rely heavily on foreign workers.

However, the Commission expects labour force growth to moderate to 3.2% in both 2027 and 2028, reflecting the projected evolution of economic activity as well as anticipated increases in work permit fees, which are expected to slow the recruitment of foreign workers.

Unemployment is forecast to remain low at 3.1% throughout the three-year period, reflecting the continued tightness of the labour market and elevated vacancy rates.

Wage growth is also expected to moderate gradually. Nominal wages increased by an average of 4.3% in 2025 and are forecast to grow by 3.9% in 2026, 3% in 2027 and 3.2% in 2028.

According to the Commission, wage growth is expected to remain at or above the rate of inflation, supporting real wage increases over the forecast period.

Inflation expected to reach 3% in 2027

Inflation is projected to rise over the coming year, reflecting higher international fuel prices and their indirect impact on the cost of imported goods and services.
