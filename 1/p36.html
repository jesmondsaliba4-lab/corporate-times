The Poppy: remembering the past, caring for the living

History records what happened. Remembrance asks us not to forget whom it happened to.

Jesmond Saliba · Malta’s Observer · Illustration: The Dispatch.pro

There is something profoundly moving about the sight of thousands of red poppies falling over Malta's War Memorial in Floriana.

As our country marked Remembrance Sunday, that image brought back memories of my schooldays, when the poppy was part of our commemorative activities. We wore it, observed moments of silence and learned about those who had given their lives in war. Perhaps, as children, we did not fully understand the suffering and sacrifice behind that small red flower. Yet its meaning stayed with us.

This year, I was also presented with a commemorative pin in my capacity as Commissioner for Voluntary Organisations. It brings together the red poppy, Malta's George Cross and the words Lest We Forget. A small gesture, but one that made me reflect on how remembrance connects our personal memories, our national history and our responsibilities today.

The poppy's association with remembrance emerged from the battlefields of Flanders during the First World War, where flowers grew amid the devastation. Immortalised in John McCrae's poem In Flanders Fields, it became a symbol of sacrifice and hope.

Yet its meaning reaches beyond Britain and the Commonwealth. In Poland, the red poppies of Monte Cassino recall the Polish soldiers who fought and died there in 1944. The song Czerwone maki na Monte Cassino became an enduring expression of their courage, sacrifice and longing for freedom.
