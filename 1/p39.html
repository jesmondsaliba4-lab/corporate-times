Confidence, once earned, must be reinforced

James Vella Clark · The editorial view of The Corporate Times · October 2026

For years, Malta’s ambition to strengthen its position as an international financial services centre has been accompanied by a persistent challenge: convincing international investors that the country offers not only opportunity, but also credibility, stability and high standards of business conduct.

Now, there are encouraging signs that this perception may finally be shifting.

According to EY’s Malta Attractiveness Survey, published this week, 81% of international executives view Malta as an attractive destination for foreign direct investment. This is the highest level of confidence recorded since 2017, offering a significant indication of how international perceptions of Malta may be evolving.

The finding could hardly have come at a better moment, as FinanceMalta prepares to host its 19th Annual Conference on 11 and 12 November.

Against a backdrop of continuing international competition and heightened expectations of transparency and good governance, this renewed vote of confidence provides a timely opportunity to reflect on how far Malta has come, and what it must do to sustain that progress.

After years in which questions surrounding Malta’s reputation have weighed on its international standing, the result offers reason for cautious optimism. It suggests that the country’s underlying strengths continue to resonate with international business leaders and that efforts to reinforce its attractiveness may be beginning to bear fruit.

Perhaps, finally, Malta’s reputation is on the mend.

However, an encouraging survey result should be viewed as a milestone, not a destination. Investor confidence is important, but the real test lies in translating that confidence into sustainable investment, quality employment, economic value and long-term growth. An attractive destination must also prove itself to be a reliable one.

This is particularly relevant to financial services, an important pillar of Malta’s economy.

The sector’s future cannot depend solely on attracting new business. Its long-term competitiveness rests on the strength of its institutions, the effectiveness of its regulatory framework and the confidence it inspires among international investors, businesses and partners.

Malta has genuine advantages: European Union membership, a skilled and English-speaking workforce, an established financial services ecosystem and extensive experience in cross-border business. Yet these strengths cannot be taken for granted. They must be supported by transparency, effective supervision, professional standards and a clear commitment to responsible business practices.

The objective must never be to attract investment at any cost, but to attract the right investment: business that creates genuine economic value, respects regulatory obligations and contributes to sustainable prosperity. Competitiveness and robust oversight are not opposing forces; in a credible financial centre, they reinforce one another.

The 81% finding is undoubtedly welcome news. But confidence, once earned, must be continuously reinforced. Reputation is built through consistent action, not a single survey result.

The challenge is now clearer. All stakeholders must continue building on this encouraging momentum by addressing the weaknesses that remain and by showing that Malta deserves the confidence international investors are increasingly prepared to place in it.

If the country is finally turning a corner, this is no time for complacency. It is time to ensure that progress continues.

James Vella Clark
