Facts first. Then the thinking.

Why a group of daily, weekly and live publications now has a monthly.

Every day, our desks publish more than any one person can read. cde.news follows the world and Malta hour by hour. The Corporate Times covers the companies, regulators and people that make up Malta’s economy. The Malta Observer reads the national evidence, from NSO tables to the Economic Survey. Comuniq.EU reads the week across Europe, every Saturday, in ten editions. And Leonardo 2050 asks the slower questions: what all of this means for people, judgement and the generation that will live with our decisions.

The Dispatch.pro is where those five streams meet, once a month.

It is a monthly for a reason. A month is long enough for a pattern to show and short enough for it still to matter. In November, Malta’s budget, the European Commission’s forecast, Moody’s rating review and a run of statements from employers, accountants, hauliers and language schools all pointed at the same question: how does an economy that has grown by adding people learn to grow by adding capability? That is our cover story.

The magazine has two halves. The first is facts: the numbers of the month, a dated record of what happened, and reports from Malta, business, technology, Europe and the world, each credited to the desk that reported it. The second is ideas: longer pieces that take a position, argue it and say where the evidence runs out.

We hold ourselves to a simple rule. A figure appears as its source published it, with the date. Where a story rests on an early or automatic signal, we say so. Where we are offering a view, we sign it.

The Dispatch.pro is a Corporate ID Group publication. It is built on what our group has always done — earned, curated and stewarded media — and on a conviction we share with ThinkPeople Nexus: technology should strengthen human judgement, not replace it.

Thank you for reading the first issue. Tell us what we missed.

Jesmond Saliba
